# Business Problem & Analysis Questions

## Problem Statement

A coffee chain wants to improve customer experience and increase revenue per order. Management needs to know **where the customer experience is weakest** — wait times and satisfaction across ordering channels — and **which factors raise the value of an order**, and **which customers generate the most revenue**, in order to guide operational improvements, upselling, and loyalty strategy.

> **Note:** This project uses a synthetic Starbucks-style dataset from Kaggle (100,000 orders, 2024–2025). It does not represent real Starbucks performance.

## Stakeholders

| Stakeholder | Focus |
|---|---|
| Operations Manager | Service speed and customer satisfaction |
| Marketing Manager | Order value, upselling, and customer loyalty |

## Analysis Questions

### Q1. Which ordering channel has the slowest service and the lowest satisfaction?
- Compare average fulfillment time and average satisfaction across the four channels (Mobile App, Drive-Thru, In-Store Cashier, Kiosk).
- Measure the share of poorly rated orders (satisfaction 1–2) in each channel.
- Follow-up: how does satisfaction change as fulfillment time increases?

**Why it matters:** identifies where the customer experience is weakest and where operational improvements should start.

### Q2. Do more complex orders take longer to fulfill?
- Compare fulfillment time across the number of customizations and the cart size.

**Why it matters:** tests the common assumption that customization slows down service, before investing in process changes based on it.

### Q3. Which factors increase order value?
- Compare average spend for orders with vs. without customizations, food items, order-ahead, and Rewards account use.
- Measure how often food is added to an order in each channel.

**Why it matters:** reveals upselling opportunities, such as encouraging food add-ons in channels where they are rarely purchased.

## Out of Scope

Region, drink category, store location type, and monthly trends are not analyzed in depth. Initial profiling showed near-identical values across these groups, which is typical of synthetic data and would not lead to meaningful business decisions.

## Data Assumptions

**Rewards membership:** `is_rewards_member` changes between orders for 93% of customers (13,873 of 14,988), so it is treated as an order-level attribute — whether a Rewards account was used on that order — rather than a fixed customer attribute. All Rewards findings are reported at order level.

### Q4. How is revenue distributed across customers by purchase frequency?
- Segment customers by number of orders and compare their share of total revenue.
- Measure the share of revenue generated by the top 10% of customers.

**Why it matters:** shows which customers a loyalty program should prioritize and how dependent revenue is on frequent buyers.
